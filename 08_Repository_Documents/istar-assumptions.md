# i* modelling assumptions

These are the assumptions behind the Strategic Dependency (SD) and Strategic
Rationale (SR) models. They are printed on the diagrams themselves.

| ID | Assumption | Evidence or reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IA-01 | The i* models were developed in Sprints 3 and 4, **after** the initial release was built, and did not inform the original design | Product backlog PB-33 to PB-35 sit in Sprints 3 and 4; PB-24 to PB-28 (the build) sit in Sprints 1 and 2 |
| IA-02 | The Clinical Nurse Specialist Administrative Support Team is subsumed into the Clinical Nurse Specialist Team | The case study attributes no distinct goal, task or resource to it |
| IA-03 | The HPAS is not modelled as an i* actor | It is a system, not an agent, and has no goals of its own; its behaviour appears as the tasks and resources that human actors rely on |
| IA-04 | External services appear only as dependees | The case study does not describe their internal behaviour, so asserting goals for them would be unsupported |
| IA-05 | Urgency is modelled as a soft goal, not a goal | The case study states the hospital has not agreed a complete set of urgency rules |
| IA-06 | Dependencies are not weighted | The model identifies risks but does not rank them; quantifying dependency strength would require operational data the initial release does not produce |
| IA-07 | Contribution links express qualitative influence only | They record whether a task helps, hurts, makes or breaks a soft goal; they carry no numeric weights |
| IA-08 | The SR opens four of the sixteen SD actors | Consultant / Clinical Team, Medical Secretaries, Treatment and Chemotherapy Bookings Team and Finance Team own the case study's hardest requirement, the separation of clinical, administrative and financial authority; opening all sixteen would make the diagram unreadable without adding rationale |
| IA-09 | Every dependency drawn in the SR also appears in the SD model | Consistency is asserted on the SR diagram and checked in Task 02, section B.5 |
